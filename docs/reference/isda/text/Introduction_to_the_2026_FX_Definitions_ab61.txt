INTRODUCTION TO THE 2026 FX DEFINITIONS

Implementation
The 2026 FX Definitions were published by ISDA and EMTA, the
trade association for emerging markets, on March 3, 2026, and are
available on ISDA’s MyLibrary platform1. They will replace the 1998
FX and Currency Option Definitions as the standard definitional
booklet for all cleared FX derivatives and new non-cleared FX
derivatives transactions from November 22, 2027.
Swift is no longer expected to support the 1998 definitions after
this date, which means all FX transactions confirmed via Swift
messages will incorporate the 2026 FX Definitions. Legacy noncleared FX derivatives transactions will continue to reference the
1998 definitions until they roll over, unless counterparties agree
bilaterally to incorporate the 2026 FX Definitions. However, it is
expected that almost 80% of legacy FX derivatives transactions will
be terminated or rolled into new positions referencing the 2026 FX
Definitions by the end of 20282.
More information is available on the FX Definitions Update
InfoHub3 on the ISDA website.

Rationale for Update
The FX market has evolved significantly since 1998. Up until
now, the terms for a particular transaction have typically been
split across a fragmented documentation framework, including the
1998 definitions, additional provisions and supplements, EMTA
template terms for non-deliverable transactions and bilateral master
confirmation agreements (MCAs).
The 2026 FX Definitions reflect current market practices and
consolidate relevant product templates and provisions published over
the past 28 years without disrupting terms in the 1998 definitions
that continue to serve the FX market. The 2026 FX Definitions

have been developed so new versions can be published from time to
time in response to future developments in the FX market. Market
participants will be able to adopt and incorporate these new versions
efficiently, avoiding the fragmentation that exists today.

Consolidation and
Harmonization Updates
Integration of Emerging Market (EM) Provisions
The 2026 FX Definitions include the EM currency matrix, which
consolidates the existing EMTA and EMTA/Singapore Foreign
Exchange Market Committee (SFEMC) template terms, including
the settlement rate options, other currency-specific terms and
applicable disruption events and disruption fallbacks within those
template terms. The 2026 FX Definitions main book also contains
provisions for cross-currency transactions and joint calculation
agent dispute resolution language, both of which have historically
been contained in MCAs for EM FX transactions, eliminating the
need for these MCAs. In addition, the 2026 FX Definitions contain
the substance of key EMTA market practice notes, making them
part of the contractual framework for EM FX transactions.

Other Consolidation and Harmonization Updates
Incorporation of Supplements and Additional Provisions: The
2026 FX Definitions main book contains FX product supplements
for barrier and binary option transactions, averaging transactions,
variance/volatility/correlation swaps, forward volatility agreement
transactions, optional early termination provisions, additional
disruption events for offshore deliverable CNY transactions and
provisions for developed market currencies.

Substantive Updates
Disruption Events and Fallbacks
Deliverable FX Transactions: Under the 2026 FX Definitions,
three disruption events and corresponding disruption fallbacks
apply automatically to deliverable FX transactions, whereas there
were no automatically applicable disruption events under the 1998
definitions. These disruption events are:
• General settlement or conversion disruption, which applies
if payment or conversion is impossible across FX market
participants generally after a waiting period.
• Material change in circumstance, which applies if a force majeure
event would prevent payment or delivery after a waiting period.
• Settlement system disruption, which applies if payment via the
applicable settlement system (eg, Continuous Linked Settlement,
known as CLS) is not possible on the relevant settlement date.
The 2026 FX Definitions also contain a fourth disruption event that
counterparties can elect to apply – specific settlement or conversion
disruption, which applies if payment or conversion is impossible
for the relevant transaction(s), irrespective of whether market
participants generally are affected.
The disruption fallbacks for deliverable FX transactions follow a
flexible approach that allows for consideration of the facts and
circumstances at the time of the disruption event. Other than for
settlement system disruption, the disruption fallbacks first include a
calculation agent determination of the settlement method, followed
by no fault termination. Counterparties could alternatively elect
to specify cash settlement as the applicable disruption fallback.
For settlement system disruption, the calculation agent takes the
rules of the applicable settlement system into account and either
postpones settlement or the counterparties settle outside of the
settlement system.
Non-deliverable FX transactions: Under the 2026 FX Definitions,
price source disruption continues to apply automatically to nondeliverable FX transactions, other than non-deliverable FX
transactions on developed market currency pairs. The definition of
this event has been updated to ensure it is only triggered when the
relevant rate is not available to market participants. The disruption
fallbacks for non-deliverable FX transactions on EM currency
pairs provide for valuation postponement and then, generally,
a calculation agent determination of the settlement rate, with
variations for certain currency pairs. These provisions are consistent
with the disruption events and disruption fallbacks currently in the
EMTA and EMTA/SFEMC template terms.

The 2026 FX Definitions incorporate specific provisions that
apply if one of the currencies in an FX transaction is ‘offshore
CNY (CNH)’ – ie, CNY traded and settled in offshore centers,
such as Hong Kong and Singapore. These provisions are based
on the additional disruption event provisions for an offshore
deliverable CNY transaction that market participants currently
use. The relevant disruption events and disruption fallbacks reflect
the unique characteristics of offshore CNY (CNH) transactions,
as distinct from CNY transactions traded onshore or on a crossborder basis.

Other Substantive Updates
Calculation Agent Standard: The calculation agent standards in the
2026 FX Definitions align with those in the 2021 ISDA Interest
Rate Derivatives Definitions and bridge a gap between English and
New York law in the existing standards. The calculation agent’s duty
is to act in “good faith using commercially reasonable procedures
to produce a commercially reasonable result”. The calculation agent
will be responsible for all determinations that are not otherwise
allocated to a party, with the exception that the calculation agent
will not determine the occurrence of a disruption event.
Calendar Adjustment Event: A calendar adjustment event is a
market event, unscheduled as of the trade date, that results in an
additional non-business day and causes the term of an FX forward to
increase by virtue of postponing the settlement date. The 2026 FX
Definitions include optional methods the parties can elect to address
economic discrepancies caused by such an event. Options include
entering into an offsetting transaction or payment of an offsetting fee.
Business Day Terms: The 2026 FX Definitions consolidate
business day definitions and conventions from various FX product
supplements published since 1998. Business day for payment
remains unchanged from the 1998 definitions, so it will continue
to point to the holiday calendar in the principal financial center
for the payment currency (or currencies). Business days for
valuation and exercise in respect of deliverable transactions and
non-deliverable transactions on developed market currencies now
incorporate concepts of spot market business day and publication
calendar day.
Full Automated Exercise: The 2026 FX Definitions introduce
an alternative exercise mechanism for deliverable, Europeanstyle options called full automated exercise. When counterparties
specify this term, it overrides automatic exercise, which remains the
default exercise method in the 2026 FX Definitions. Full automated
exercise relies on published rates (as opposed to the spot market)
and removes manual intervention in the exercise/expiry process,
including exercise notifications from buyers to sellers.
